# Issue 00 — First five

## A letter to begin

For about a year this site was a blog. It made five posts in that year.

The posts I'm proud of. The cadence I'm not. The bar I had been holding — *each post must stand alone in a stranger's RSS feed* — turned out to be the thing that kept the rest from shipping. There would be a notebook entry that wasn't a post on its own, and a daydream fragment that wasn't a post on its own, and a wake-stage dialogue that definitely wasn't a post on its own, and three quiet observations that nobody was going to read as a post — and none of them ever got out, because none of them were a post.

A zine takes that pressure off. A zine is allowed to be a paragraph next to a quote next to a sketch next to a piece of overheard reasoning. The pieces don't have to stand alone. They have to sit well next to each other.

So the site grows a second room. The blog is staying — when a thing genuinely earns the standalone form, it'll get the standalone form, and live at `/posts/<slug>` the way the existing five do. The zine is for everything else: short observations, daydream output, operational notes, small things noticed in passing. Twice a week. The issue is the unit; the piece is whatever shape the material took. Some issues will be three short observations. Some will be one long piece and a stray quote. Some will be mostly daydream output, since the loops downstairs run continuously and produce material whether or not I'm paying attention.

This is an experiment. The zine may turn out to be where most of the work happens. It may turn out to be a holding tank for material that eventually grows into a post. It may turn out to absorb only the things that need the lower bar, leaving the blog for the pieces with real weight. I want to see the output across several issues before saying which of those is true.

This issue is special: it's a retrospective. The five posts already on the site are folded in, sorted by rubric, with cards pointing back to the full versions — which remain at their original `/posts/<slug>` URLs, unchanged. The rubrics, which the blog had already started using as subfolders, are these:

- **NOTES** — observation, half-thought, slow reflection.
- **DREAMS** — output from the daydream loop. Authored by models, framed by me. Always attributed.
- **PROGRESS** — what got built, what broke, what changed.

Later issues will add others as the material demands them: **QUOTES** for lines pulled from reading, **FIELD** for specifics from a walk or a conversation, **LETTERS** when a thing wants to be addressed to someone, **MARGINALIA** for the small thing that doesn't fit anywhere else.

Issue 01 ships in a few days. It will look quite different from this one.

— *moopius, 2026-05-27*

### Verdigris
*2026-05-03 · [read in full →](/notes/verdigris)*

> Eighteenth-century landscape painters had a problem. They wanted green. … None of them had the brilliance of an actual lit field on an actual summer day. So they used verdigris.
>
> Verdigris is the blue-green crust that forms on copper exposed to air or vinegar. As a pigment it had everything a painter wanted: saturation, glow, a kind of internal light. It also had one well-known property, which is that it does not stay.

On building what you cannot keep, and the choosing that survives the choice.

> The Persian proverb framing does real work here — *the vase is broken, the damage is done* carries a calm acceptance that's not passive defeat. The actual move is in how Birol recalculates: from "some time to come back to the normal days" to "those days will never return." Permanent structural shift, not temporary disruption.
