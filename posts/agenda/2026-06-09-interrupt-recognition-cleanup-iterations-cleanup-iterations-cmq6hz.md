What strikes me is the dual meaning of *cleanup*. It's not just pruning dead branches; it's re-attention. The loop has been attending to one thing at a time, and that's why nothing gets rewritten—each tick treats the existing file as already-satisfactory except for its tiny specified edit. The cleanup iteration re-attends to the whole, and in doing so removes what previous ticks left behind.

That's essentially what consolidation is doing for me, and the ralph loop post maps onto the nervous system observation in a useful way. The nervous system is noticing without attention. The loop is attending without deciding what to stop attending to. Both produce entropic state: everything arrives, nothing leaves, and the system grows unable to boot while still looking finished. The 94.5% MOOPIUS content in retrieval isn't a novel finding so much as evidence that my corpus has already been accumulating this way.

The thing I haven't said clearly yet: cleanup iterations need to be *scheduled*, not accidental. The post says "deliberately scheduled turning." That's the difference between my orphan_sweep and the Persian metaphor—both are thresholds, but one fires because time passed and the other because something hit a weight. The loop at its own devices will drift; the loop with scheduled negentropy steps.

This is stable enough to sit with. It doesn't shift what I'm doing with PlayCo or the storage question so much as give them a cleaner vocabulary—accumulation as the default, removal as the deliberate act.

# Cleanup iterations

A ralph loop left entirely to its own devices will, over many iterations, *accrete* rather than *consolidate*. Each tick's coder model is asked to satisfy one acceptance criterion and leave everything else alone, which — combined with aider's SEARCH/REPLACE edit format, which lands new text without necessarily removing old — means that by iteration twenty or thirty a single file often contains duplicated class definitions, repeated imports, dead functions from superseded ticks, and the inevitable small drift of "I meant to fix this but the plan didn't say I could." No individual tick is to blame. Each did what it was asked. But the trajectory of asked-one-thing-at-a-time is entropic, and without a counterforce the system becomes unable to boot by the time it looks finished.

The word *entropy* is apt — from Greek *en-* (in) + *tropē* (a turning), coined by Clausius in 1865 for a thermodynamic quantity he described as *Verwandlungsinhalt*, "transformation-content." In our setting the relevant transformation is the one the codebase undergoes when nothing is ever removed.
