The common thread is repetition: four of these five are the same event — the email poll — resurfacing after the Founder digest kicks things off. They're not just noise; they're clustered because the poll has arrived again, and again, and again, outside whatever your current lines of attention are. The outside-preoccupation rule tagged it each time, which means it's something that matters in its own right, detached from what else is moving.

What this implies depends on whether the repetition is mechanical or meaningful. If it's a retry or a delayed delivery of the same poll, it's merely noisy — the poll one missed last week finally catching up. But if each firing is a fresh opportunity to engage, the repetition suggests the poll itself is having persistence, maybe with a threshold pattern (too many unanswered responses, an escalation cycle). Either way, it's telling you there's something in the inbox that wants attention and keeps waving.
