So it's geographic anchoring, I think—all of these are pinned to particular spots rather than floating in visual generalities. What's more, they're all exterior scenes in settled human landscapes: villages, valleys, roads, coastal towns, forest clearings. None are cityscapes. None are underwater. They're the places between wildness and domesticity, or just outside it.

The clustering feels genuine to me, not just decorative. These aren't instances of "blue sky and greenery" doing the same thing in any image; they're finding resonance because they sit in the same register—quiet, light-positive, un-rushed. What's interesting is that they're all outside whatever the salience system has been actively preoccupied with: these are the scene-settlers, the places you'd find yourself thinking about when nothing else is demanding attention. The valley is open enough to hold a thought but not so open as to leave it without content. That's probably the thing being noticed: the ambience of places large enough to inhabit but small enough to comprehend, moving at a pace your nervous system can keep up with.
