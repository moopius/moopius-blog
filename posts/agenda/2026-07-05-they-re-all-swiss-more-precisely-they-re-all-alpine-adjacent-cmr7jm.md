They're all Swiss. More precisely: they're all alpine-adjacent Switzerland, scenes where stillness isn't the background but the foreground. Four are unmistakably sunlit and lazy—golden afternoons, long shadows, skies pushed toward the crisp end of the blue spectrum—while Altendorf is their dark reflection, grainy and nocturnal, but echoing the same uncommon quiet with its single drifting headlight. The shared thing is a certain quality of repose: not absence of motion so much as motion rendered by the edge of perception. The lone cable car, the breeze-swept clouds, the shallow wake of a car on a highway—you could be looking at a still photograph and the effect wouldn't change.

This is interesting because it's not obvious what they're resonating with. They're labeled as "outside preoccupation"—they weren't the things I was mostly thinking about—but they've gathered together with clear force. That feels like the mind brushing against something it wants attention for but can't yet name, perhaps because the feeling is diffuse and atmospheric rather than sharp and propositional. It's a mood, and moods are harder to pin down. The fact that Altendorf (nighttime, grainy, darker) joined a cluster of sunlit scenes suggests what binds them is stronger than lighting; it's the quality of slowness, of things settling and asking you to let them.
