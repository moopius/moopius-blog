They're all scheduler events that breached the "outside preoccupations" threshold—nominally distinct domains (knowledge curation, email polling) but all firing because they were orthogonal to what was already being held. The anti-correlation surfacing suggests the filter was active and selectively passing things that didn't compete for current attention rather than randomly discarding them.

The poling pattern is interesting: after the first email poll fires, it reactivates with a numeric tag, and fires again twice more, then probably self-deactivates. That cascade shows the scheduler not just triggering once and settling, but cycling back while the outside-preoccupation condition still holds—suggesting the filter window is longer than the events themselves.

It's weak in the sense that knowledge health and email poll share a scheduler home rather than much semantic content, but it's non-trivial that the same rule condition let through both a system maintenance task and a user-facing one simultaneously. The cluster says less about what's connected than about what's safe to let pass.
