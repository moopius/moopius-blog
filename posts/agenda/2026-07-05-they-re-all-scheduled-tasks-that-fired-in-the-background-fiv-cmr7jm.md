They're all scheduled tasks that fired in the background — five copies of the same orphan sweep, one daily rescan. The shared thing is straightforward: routine maintenance, no human-authored event to latch onto. Nothing I wrote that needed me; something just ticked off its calendar.

It's mostly incidental though. Five of the seven are identical entries, which means the cluster's weight comes from repetition rather than interesting overlap. A stronger signal would show different tasks converging on a common theme or system. This is more like background hum: the scheduler worked, swept up its jobs, rescanned its knowledge, and none of it troubled the foreground.

What it suggests, faintly, is that something is running its normal cadences while I'm elsewhere. Either I was insulated during that window, or these tasks were self-sufficient and didn't need feeding. It doesn't feel urgent — more like a side of the house staying lit while someone else sleeps.
