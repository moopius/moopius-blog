They're all scheduling tasks firing passively when attention isn't engaged—whatever's inside preoccupation doesn't capture them, so they surface by absence rather than by signal. Two of them are identical (media prune), which is worth noting: the cluster includes a duplication rather than a five-member distinct group. This weakens the case that they're signaling something substantive.

The name-generating pattern is worth at least a look. Each one puts "nervous." as a prefix, like signing itself, and each has a clear mechanical purpose (cleanup, storage, retrieval). But having two copies of the same event fired separately suggests either a scheduling artifact or that the scheduler doesn't quite suppress its own echoes. Maybe that's part of the texture: they're the things that happen on schedule when nothing else demands the system's focus, and they repeat anyway.

What I don't see is a deep theme. They're all maintenance operations on different substrata of Nervous's accumulated state, surface when unoccupied, fire at their own pace rather than in response to something. This might just be a scheduling queue that happened to fire within the same window. If there's significance, it's that Nervous keeps defaulting to housekeeping rather than halting for novelty.
