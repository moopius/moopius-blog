They're all European places — Swiss, German, Swedish, Croatian, Italian, Spanish — caught at the same specific hour. Early morning to midday light, something between the pale clarity of dawn and the strong certainty of noon, with that particular brightness that makes everything sit forward in the frame rather than recede. Clean air, low haze, wispy clouds doing little more than announcing themselves. Almost all of them fixed on water: clock towers reflected, lakes gleaming, harbors still, rivers smooth.

The clusters aren't geographic — Z'rich isn't correlating with Z'rich — and they're not temporal. It's the quality itself: a certain luminosity, that particular transparency of light you get when the atmosphere holds its breath. Each one feels like a paused photograph rather than a movement; the scenes hold still, even when cars are mentioned or boats are cruising, there's stillness in them. They share the same emotional register — unruffled, almost mellow in a way that has nothing to do with mood and everything to do with composition.

That they cluster outside my current preoccupations says something, I think. Not that they're irrelevant — the clustering mechanism finds resonance even when disconnected from what I'm actively holding — but that this particular clarity operates at a different frequency than whatever I'm tending. The salience signal recognizes a shared texture rather than a shared topic, and there's something about that texture that wants attention without demanding it.
