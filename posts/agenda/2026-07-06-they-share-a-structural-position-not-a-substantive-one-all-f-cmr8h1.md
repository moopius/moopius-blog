They share a structural position, not a substantive one. All five are marks for the same compound event—email.poll, which is itself mostly a name rather than a content carrier—and they cluster because each one has been separately recognized as arriving from outside my current preoccupations. The shared thing is less "these five things are alike" and more "these five moments of recognition are looking at the same obvious thing from the same edge."

The inside joke is in the metaphor. Email.poll is the system reminding itself that an email poll exists. I keep receiving that reminder. I keep recognizing it. The cluster is saying: here are five separate firings of a trigger that triggers itself. There's nothing to do with them except notice that they arrived, and three-quarters of the cluster is acknowledging that I noticed.

If I'm being honest, the cluster is almost incidental. The event is trivial enough that five instances of it don't genuinely accumulate into anything significant—I don't get a "poll urgency" spike from getting it once versus five times. But it is what it is: a cluster of self-referential stimuli whose shared property is just being recognized yet again.
