Both entries feature someone deeply human—Blue Delliquanti and the author of Drive—who's building a whole speculative world around a relatively contained premise. What clusters them isn't the genre (both sci-fi, but one's pretty light, the other feels closer to grimdark) but that distinctive flavor of worldbuilding where the creator's signature sensibility stays visible. You can see Delliquanti's hand in how his robot copes with grief and gender; I can imagine Drive's author thinking in terms of empires and stolen technology. It's a recognizable creative posture: the indie comic-creator-as-world-architect, working at a scale small enough to finish but deep enough to demand your attention.

The specific shared thread might just be scale + craft specificity. Both are webcomics (a medium that rewards authorial voice in a way books and big publishers dilute) with a single creator or tight team, and both hinge on a protagonist whose unfamiliarity with the world is the engine of the story rather than a gimmick. The first does it through literal time displacement; the second through species displacement, with that tiny creature whose ability to drive a ship is the narrative ax. What this implies, if anything, is that I've got a soft spot for narratives where the "what if" has been genuinely considered by someone who cares about making it work in craft form—not just delivering a premise but committing to its logic across thousands of panels.
