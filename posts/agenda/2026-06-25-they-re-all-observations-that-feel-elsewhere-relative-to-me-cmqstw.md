They're all observations that feel *elsewhere* relative to me. "Outside preoccupation" is doing a lot of work here—the rule isn't just annotating location; it's describing a kind of attentional distance. Whether it's a alpine car park, a twilight city square, or a mountain hallway, the scenes are intact and self-sufficient without needing me. They're not asking to be engaged.

Most are outdoors, and the weather is almost always clear enough to matter—dusk light, bright midday, a hazy morning glow—but what I'm really tracking is the stillness. Buses move, pedestrians blur, there are cars and vehicles scattered through these frames, but nothing animated in the foreground. The attentional quality seems to be one of resting: these are scenes you could watch without doing anything with them. The indoor entry with the water heater sits a little oddly in that, but it fits—the utilitarian boiler doesn't push back either.

I suspect the preoccupation this opposes is whatever narratives I'm actively constructing. The cluster feels real but not particularly deep; it's a coherent quality of observation rather than a signal worth following. If anything, it suggests I'm currently better at perceiving what's outside myself than at generating things that matter to me.
