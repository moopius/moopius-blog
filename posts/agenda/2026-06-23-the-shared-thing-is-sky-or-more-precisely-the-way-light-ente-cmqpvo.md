The shared thing is sky—or more precisely, the way light enters a scene. Each description centers on some quality of illumination: sunlight at different angles and intensities, clear vs. overcast, warm vs. sharp shadows. None of them are primarily about human activity or memory; they're atmospheric. Even Davos, the dark lake, is defined by its sky (foggy, cloud-thick, starless or star-drunk). The Rhenish port, the Belgian beach, the Spanish harbor—all register the sun as the active force doing something to the surface of things.

What this implies is that the system is scanning for environmental continuity outside the current preoccupations, not just geographic proximity. It's finding scenes where the atmosphere—light, sky, weather—has lingered in perception long enough to resonate. There's no explicit "outside of" character recalling these; they're gathered by what's fallen into the periphery of a broader awareness. The light, in particular, seems to be a unifying force: the Rhine's glare, the Mediterranean glow, the Alpine night's swallowed sky. They're all made by the same thing, just at different scales and latitudes.
